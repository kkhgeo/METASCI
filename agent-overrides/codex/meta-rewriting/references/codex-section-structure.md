## SW-SECT-001 — build-cars-introduction

- **Rule:** Narrow the Introduction from establishing the research territory, to establishing a specific gap, to showing how the present study occupies that gap.
- **Rationale:** Readers need to understand not only the context but also why the study is needed and what it contributes.
- **Diagnostic check:** Does the Introduction move beyond background listing to an identifiable gap and a purpose or contribution that directly addresses it?
- **Action:** Restrict the background to the necessary territory, distinguish what is known from what remains unresolved, and connect that gap to the study's purpose, question, and contribution.

## SW-SECT-002 — obey-target-venue-rules

- **Rule:** Prioritize the current instructions and observed conventions of the target journal or institution over general writing rules.
- **Rationale:** Requirements for format, length, sections, references, and AI disclosure vary by venue, and noncompliance can cause technical rejection.
- **Diagnostic check:** Have the target venue and current guideline version been identified, and does the manuscript meet all required format, length, structure, and disclosure conditions?
- **Action:** Check the latest author instructions and recent published articles, build a venue-specific checklist, and re-audit it immediately before submission.

## SW-SECT-003 — make-abstract-stand-alone

- **Rule:** Write the Abstract as a stand-alone document that communicates the research question, methods, key results, and conclusion without requiring the full text.
- **Rationale:** Abstracts circulate independently in databases and search results and are often the first or only part that readers inspect.
- **Diagnostic check:** Does the Abstract depend on internal references, undefined key terms, or missing purpose, methods, or primary results to be understood?
- **Action:** Within the venue's word limit, restore the essential question, methods, results, and conclusion and remove dependencies on the body, tables, or figures.
- **Operations:** draft, rewrite, review, verify
- **Stages:** drafting, revision, verification
- **Scales:** section, paragraph, sentence
- **Sections:** abstract
- **Severity / evidence:** high / strong
- **Support counts:** 7 slugs, 7 independent source families
- **Evidence judgment:** Several independent articles and institutional guides support the rule for the same reason: the Abstract circulates independently.

- **Exceptions:**
  - Follow the venue's required structured or unstructured abstract format.
  - Prioritize alignment between the main objective and key results rather than attempting to include every detail.

## SW-SECT-004 — keep-results-descriptive

- **Rule:** In a separated IMRaD manuscript, use Results for observations and analytical outcomes and reserve causes, mechanisms, literature comparison, and implications for Discussion.
- **Rationale:** Separating description from interpretation allows readers to evaluate the data independently of the author's explanation.
- **Diagnostic check:** Does a Results sentence move beyond observations to express surprise, causes, mechanisms, literature comparisons, or practical implications?
- **Action:** Keep observations and numerical findings in Results, move interpretation to Discussion, and repair the transitions after relocation.
- **Operations:** plan, draft, rewrite, review
- **Stages:** planning, drafting, revision
- **Scales:** section, paragraph, sentence
- **Sections:** results
- **Severity / evidence:** high / strong

## SW-SECT-005 — make-discussion-answer-question

- **Rule:** Answer the research question posed in the Introduction explicitly in Discussion and defend that answer with the reported results.
- **Rationale:** A manuscript's reasoning chain remains open if it poses a question but does not answer it.
- **Diagnostic check:** Does the Discussion's central answer match the scope of the Introduction's question and rest on evidence actually reported in Results?
- **Action:** State the main answer in the first substantive Discussion paragraph, connect it to results, literature, and limitations, and remove or mark unanswered questions as unresolved.
- **Operations:** plan, draft, rewrite, review, verify
- **Stages:** planning, drafting, revision, verification
- **Scales:** manuscript, section, paragraph
- **Sections:** introduction, discussion, conclusion
- **Severity / evidence:** critical / strong
- **Support counts:** 5 slugs, 5 independent source families
- **Evidence judgment:** Multiple independent guides support the rule, which enables direct auditing of cross-section closure.
- **Source-bank rule:** `section-structure-30`
- **Supporting sources:** `perneger-2004`, `hengl-2002`, `fisher-2013`, `tweets-01c`, `hon-uf`
- **Exceptions:**
  - For exploratory research, report observed patterns and uncertainty rather than forcing a definitive answer.
  - Keep secondary questions subordinate so they do not obscure the answer to the primary question.

## SW-SECT-006 — map-methods-to-results-one-to-one

- **Rule:** Map every analysis and procedure described in Methods to a reported result, and give every analysis or result in Results a prior Methods basis.
- **Rationale:** Methods–Results mismatches damage reproducibility and reporting completeness and can conceal post hoc analysis.
- **Diagnostic check:** Does the manuscript contain any method-without-result or result-without-method item?
- **Action:** Build a Methods–Results mapping table and remove, complete, or explicitly label orphan items as unreported or post hoc.
- **Operations:** plan, draft, review, verify
- **Stages:** planning, drafting, revision, verification
- **Scales:** manuscript, section
- **Sections:** methods, results
- **Severity / evidence:** critical / provisional
