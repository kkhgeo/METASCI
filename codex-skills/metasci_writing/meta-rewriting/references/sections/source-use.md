# References and Source Use

Use with [the shared principles](../principles.md), including §§6.3–6.4 on available context, preservation, and disclosure. Apply section-level completeness checks only when the relevant section is supplied.

**Basis notes:** Original IDs point to the archived guide linked below. They identify practical guidance and provenance, not newly verified empirical findings. “Editorial” identifies a new diagnostic or qualification in this revision. Diagnostic questions are aids to judgment, not a mandatory sequence.

## Purpose

Make borrowed evidence traceable and represent it accurately. This guide applies across sections.

## 1. Source selection and representation

### SO1. Select for relevance and evidential fit

Choose sources that support consequential claims, adopted methods, and borrowed material. Original studies may support specific findings; reviews may support synthesis. Assess the actual source, not just its language, publication type, or reputation.

Authoritative reports, datasets, standards, and technical documentation may provide the appropriate evidence in environmental and policy research.

**Fit check:** What claim does this source support, and why is it suitable for that claim?

**Basis:** Original section-structure-14 and -63; remined-references-8 and -10. Relevance-based reconciliation and diagnostic: editorial.

### SO2. Preserve what the source actually establishes

Retain the source’s scope, conditions, and uncertainty. Keep the author’s own findings and interpretations distinguishable from borrowed ones.

**Placement check:** Can the reader identify which proposition a citation supports? For grouped citations, check whether they support the stated shared proposition or contribute different evidence that should be explained.

**Basis:** Original section-structure-16; remined-references-8 and -9; editorial diagnostic.

## 2. Review depth

### SO3. Distinguish three checks

- **Placement:** Is the citation’s role in the sentence clear?
- **Bibliographic identity:** Does the source exist, with accurate identifying details?
- **Content support:** Does the inspected source material support the proposed claim?

State the level performed. Preserving an existing citation during a language edit does not verify its content. Do not expand a wording review into a full citation audit unless requested or necessary to resolve a substantive concern.

**Basis:** Original section-structure-19; remined-references-13; editorial review-level distinction.

### SO4. Inspect enough original material for the claim

Use `Knowledge/` extracts and summaries to locate evidence. Record what was actually inspected; a summary is not a full-text check.

Before presenting a newly added citation as support, confirm its identity and the relevant source content. An abstract may support a broad finding it explicitly reports. Particular values, procedures, subgroup findings, or causal interpretations may require the relevant full-text passage, table, or supplement.

**Sufficiency check:** Does the inspected material establish the claim at the level of detail used in the manuscript? Whole-paper reading is not automatically necessary, but missing qualifiers must not be ignored.

If support is unavailable, mark the proposed addition as needing evidence. Verify exact quotations against the original passage. Identify secondary accounts as such when the original cannot be accessed.

**Basis:** Original section-structure-19; remined-references-13. Claim-specific inspection and diagnostic: editorial.

## 3. Identification and format

### SO5. Make the source locatable

Provide accurate details appropriate to its type and the target style. Prefer stable identifiers and precise source links. Check versions of changing documents when currency matters.

Reference managers help with organization and formatting; they do not establish evidential support.

Treat reproduction permissions according to the source’s license and publication requirements when reused material is part of the task.

**Basis:** Original section-structure-19; remined-references-1–7, -11, -12, reconciled editorially as style-dependent guidance; original Results section-structure-64 for reused visuals.
