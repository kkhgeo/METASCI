## SW-PROC-001 — treat-revision-as-default

- **Rule:** Treat every first draft as an intermediate artifact and include revision as a default stage of the writing process.
- **Rationale:** A first draft externalizes thought and evidence; iterative revision refines its logic, focus, and expression.
- **Diagnostic check:** Is a first draft being treated as final or submission-ready without a global revision and a local editing pass?
- **Action:** Label the artifact as a draft and schedule at least one global revision followed by one local editing pass.
- **Operations:** draft, rewrite, review

## SW-PROC-002 — outline-before-drafting

- **Rule:** Define the argumentative skeleton of sections and paragraphs before drafting continuous prose.
- **Rationale:** An outline exposes omissions, duplication, and ordering problems before sentence-level drafting creates avoidable rework.
- **Diagnostic check:** Has each section and paragraph been assigned a function and a one-sentence claim before prose drafting begins?
- **Action:** State each section purpose and paragraph claim, settle their order and evidence placement, and only then draft prose.
- **Operations:** plan, draft

## SW-PROC-003 — define-one-research-question

- **Rule:** Define one clear research question or core message before drafting and use it as the manuscript's selection criterion.
- **Rationale:** A clear question allows the introduction's gap, the methods, the results, and the discussion's answer to form one reasoning chain.
- **Diagnostic check:** Can the central question be stated in one sentence, and does every major section contribute to answering it?
- **Action:** Fix the question or core message in one sentence; remove, relocate, or reserve for another manuscript any point that does not directly contribute.
- **Operations:** plan, draft, review
- **Stages:** planning, drafting, revision
- **Scales:** process, manuscript
- **Sections:** all
- **Severity / evidence:** high / strong
- **Support counts:** 9 slugs, 7 independent source families
- **Evidence judgment:** Independent articles and institutional guides repeatedly support this prerequisite for a coherent research-argument chain.
- **Source-bank rule:** `process-7`

- **Exceptions:**
  - For multiple research questions, define their hierarchy and mappings under one explicit higher-level question.
  - For exploratory research, define the exploratory purpose and analytical scope instead of forcing a confirmatory hypothesis.

## SW-PROC-004 — revise-global-before-local

- **Rule:** Resolve global problems in logic, flow, and focus before editing local problems in grammar, formatting, and wording.
- **Rationale:** Polishing text that will later move or disappear wastes effort, and local fluency can conceal structural defects.
- **Diagnostic check:** Is grammar or wording being polished before the structure and argument have been stabilized?
- **Action:** Fix the question chain, paragraph functions, sequence, and evidence placement before moving to sentence-level editing.
- **Operations:** rewrite, review
- **Stages:** revision
- **Scales:** process, manuscript, section, paragraph
- **Sections:** all
- **Severity / evidence:** high / moderate
- **Support counts:** 6 slugs, 4 independent source families
- **Evidence judgment:** Several guide families support the rule, but the exact staging remains a procedural heuristic that depends on manuscript and task.

- **Supporting sources:** `hengl-2002`, `hon-uf`, `b3-lund`, `tweets-01a`, `tweets-01d`, `tweets-02`
- **Exceptions:**
  - If grammatical damage blocks interpretation, make the minimum repair needed to understand the passage first.
  - If the user explicitly requests surface editing only, respect scope and flag structural risks separately.
