# Academic Paragraph Review and Rewrite

Diagnose before rewriting. Improve reasoning, information flow, and expression while preserving the author’s facts and intended meaning.

Write explanations and diagnoses in Korean. Provide the revised paragraph in English, followed by a faithful Korean translation. For manuscript prose that should remain Korean, use `meta-rewriting-korean`.

For a Korean draft intended for English, translate and revise together. Do not add relationships or conditions absent from the original; preserve unresolved ambiguity or flag it for clarification.

## 1. Understand the input

Identify the target paragraph, its section, and the requested work from the supplied text, files, and conversation. Ask only when uncertainty about the target or section materially affects the review.

Review one paragraph at a time by default. Honor the user’s selected paragraph; otherwise start with the first. Use surrounding paragraphs as context.

If the project has a `Knowledge/` directory, consult relevant literature or extracted notes when needed to understand the background or assess a literature-based claim.

## 2. Load the relevant guidance

Read `references/principles.md` and the guide for the paragraph’s section:

| Section | Guide |
|---|---|
| Title, abstract, or keywords | `references/sections/title-abstract.md` |
| Introduction or literature review | `references/sections/introduction.md` |
| Methods | `references/sections/methods.md` |
| Results | `references/sections/results.md` |
| Discussion | `references/sections/discussion.md` |
| Conclusion | `references/sections/conclusion.md` |

For combined Results and Discussion, use both guides. If the section remains unknown, apply the shared principles and state the scope limitation.

Read `references/sections/source-use.md` when assessing source support or adding or changing citations. Distinguish extracted notes from original source material.

Reuse guidance already read in the conversation unless it has changed or needs clarification. Apply section requirements according to the paragraph’s role; do not demand every section element within one paragraph.

## 3. Diagnose and decide

Briefly summarize the paragraph’s intended claim and function. Treat this as an interpretation the author can correct.

Examine every sentence in order and in context. Assess reasoning, support, information flow, and expression before drafting improvements.

Report findings for affected sentences in sentence order. Quote the problematic wording, explain its consequence, and describe the repair direction. Explain the sentence’s role when useful. Cite principle identifiers, such as §2.3, when they clarify the basis of a judgment.

Group sentences without findings into one line: “수정 불필요: 문장 1, 4, 6.” If the user requests commentary on every sentence, provide it.

Then identify paragraph-level problems requiring more than a local repair, such as missing logical links, misplaced material, or redundancy. Avoid repeating the sentence diagnoses.

Distinguish substantive defects from stylistic preferences and unavailable context. Do not invent problems to justify a rewrite. Substantive defects include expression errors that obstruct meaning, as well as problems in reasoning and information flow.

Finish with **리라이팅 권고** or **원문 유지**, with a brief reason. Recommend keeping the original when there are no substantive defects and possible changes concern stylistic preference alone. Omit rewriting in that case unless the user explicitly requests a translation or revised version; fulfill that request even when the original is effective. For diagnosis-only requests, stop here.

## 4. Rewrite and explain

When rewriting is warranted or explicitly requested, produce one complete revised paragraph. For a sound Korean draft requiring English translation, preserve its effective argument and structure.

Choose the extent of revision from the diagnosis. Preserve effective wording and structure; reorder, combine, split, or reconstruct material when needed. Neither minimal change nor extensive rewriting is a goal in itself.

Explain substantive changes briefly in Korean, then provide the English revision and its Korean translation. For substantial restructuring, explain why local edits would leave the problem unresolved.

Apply `references/principles.md` §6.4 for preservation and disclosure. Mark unsupported proposed additions with `[evidence needed]` and translate the marker as `[근거 필요]`.

Identify unresolved choices about meaning rather than silently selecting an unsupported interpretation. Provide multiple versions only when the user requests alternatives or comparison.

## Default response

1. Intended claim and paragraph role
2. Findings for affected sentences; one line identifying sentences needing no change
3. Paragraph-level findings, when present, and the rewriting decision
4. If proceeding: substantive change notes, one English revision, and its Korean translation

A table is optional. Briefly identify the guidance applied and the extent of any source checking. State unresolved substantive questions where they affect the revision.

## Follow-up requests

- **Diagnosis only:** Return the diagnosis and decision without rewriting.
- **Less or more revision:** Produce one adjusted version in the requested direction, with an updated translation.
- **Partial revision:** Change only the requested portion and return the updated paragraph and translation.
- **Comparison:** Provide the requested alternatives and explain their substantive differences.
- **Adopt the revision:** Return the English paragraph and its Korean translation, ready to copy.
- **Keep the original:** Acknowledge the decision without further rewriting.
- **Next paragraph:** Continue with the next supplied target, reusing available context.

## Completion

Confirm that every sentence has been assessed and that the revision addresses the diagnosed problems.

Compare the original and revision item by item for numerical values, units, uncertainty estimates, citations, and figure or table identifiers. Check that citations remain attached to the intended claims. Disclose intentional substantive omissions, additions, and changes in scope, certainty, or causal meaning.

Ensure the Korean translation matches the English revision, including qualifications and evidence-needed markers. Do not describe preserved citations as verified unless their source support was actually checked.
