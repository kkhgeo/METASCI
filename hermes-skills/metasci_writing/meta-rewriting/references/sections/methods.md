# Methods

Use with [the shared principles](../principles.md), including §§6.3–6.4 on available context, preservation, and disclosure. Apply section-level completeness checks only when the relevant section is supplied.

**Basis notes:** Original IDs point to the archived guide linked below. They identify practical guidance and provenance, not newly verified empirical findings. “Editorial” identifies a new diagnostic or qualification in this revision. Diagnostic questions are aids to judgment, not a mandatory sequence.

## Purpose

Explain what was done sufficiently for a knowledgeable reader to understand, assess, and, where feasible, reproduce the study.

## 1. Procedure and rationale

### ME1. Make the analytical chain clear

Organize the account around the actual workflow or analytical structure. Connect study design, observations, preparation, measurements, models, and analyses as relevant.

**Procedure check:** Can the reader identify the inputs, the operation performed, and the resulting data or estimate? Distinguish measured quantities from derived ones.

Explain unusual choices and meaningful departures from cited methods.

**Basis:** Original section-structure-13, -57, -61; remined-methods-4, -7, -9. Diagnostic: editorial.

### ME2. Report consequential detail

Include details whose absence could lead a reader to perform a materially different procedure or interpret the result differently.

Depending on the study, these may include sampling locations and times, inclusion rules, instrument settings, quality control, transformations, missing-data treatment, assumptions, model parameters, calibration, uncertainty methods, and software versions.

**Omission check:** Name the plausible alternative procedure or interpretation caused by the missing detail. If none can be identified, the detail may be optional rather than a substantive defect.

Use supplementary material for extensive detail while retaining enough in the main text to understand the analysis.

**Basis:** Original section-structure-13, -60; remined-methods-2, -10, -11. Detail-selection criterion and expanded examples: editorial.

### ME3. Connect methods and reported analyses

Every reported analysis needs an explained method, either here or in an appropriate cited or linked description. Supporting procedures need not each produce a separate reported result.

State adaptations to established methods. Report actual procedures and relevant deviations rather than idealized recommendations.

**Basis:** Original section-structure-61; editorial qualification of the original one-to-one requirement.

## 2. Scope of diagnosis

### ME4. Separate reporting defects from study concerns

A reporting defect means the account does not explain what was done. A design or analysis concern questions the adequacy of the work itself.

**Repair check:** Could accurate clarification of the existing procedure resolve the problem? If the proposed repair requires new sampling, analysis, validation, or a changed design, identify it as a study concern rather than silently rewriting it as completed work.

**Basis:** Editorial diagnostic boundary; informed by original reproducibility guidance.

### ME5. Apply study-specific criteria selectively

Use the relevant criteria for the actual study: ethics and registration for applicable research; search and synthesis for systematic reviews; methodological approach for qualitative work; measurement suitability for instruments; inputs and evaluation for modelling.

When compliance checking is requested, consult the applicable reporting standard. Report data and code availability in the manuscript’s designated location, which may be outside Methods.

**Basis:** Original section-structure-43, -58, -59; remined-methods-9, -10, -14–17. Routing and availability placement: editorial.

## 3. Paragraph review

### ME6. Assess one procedure in context

Identify the procedure explained by the paragraph and its connection to adjacent steps. Flag a missing parameter only after checking supplied surrounding material.

Choose voice and subsection order for clarity. Proposal-specific schedules, anticipated pitfalls, and fallback plans apply when reviewing a proposal, not as additions to completed-study reporting.

**Basis:** Original remined-methods-1, -3–8, -18; editorial scope qualification.
