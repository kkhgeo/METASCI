### `section-structure-13` — convergence 9
Write Methods with enough specific, technical detail that a competent researcher with the same resources could reproduce the study.

> "The Methods section should provide the readers with sufficient detail about the study methods to be able to reproduce the study if so desired. Thus, this section should be specific, concrete, technical, and fairly detailed."

**Sources (9):** perneger-2004, hengl-2002, fisher-2013, b5-schulzrinne, hon-uf, tweets-01a, b3-lund, b1-iufro, ecarnot-2015
**Scope:** Methods

### `section-structure-61` — convergence 1
Methods and Results must map one-to-one — no result for a test not described in Methods, and a result for every method described; previously published procedures need only a brief summary plus a reference.

> "There must be a method described for every result you intend to include in your results section – i.e., you cannot present the results of a test or analysis that was not mentioned in the methods. Conversely, if details of any or all procedures have previously been published elsewhere, then a brief summary will suffice, accompanied by a reference to the relevant publication."

**Sources (1):** ecarnot-2015
**Scope:** Methods · also filed under: 04_results.md
**[v] Verified 2026-08-09** — sole source `ecarnot-2015`; quote confirmed verbatim against the source PDF.

### `remined-methods-4` — convergence 1

> "it is useful to consider the three questions of \"how?\", \"when?\", and \"why?\" when describing your methods."

### `remined-methods-9` — convergence 1
