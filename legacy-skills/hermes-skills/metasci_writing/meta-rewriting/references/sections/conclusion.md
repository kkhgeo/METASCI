# Conclusion

Use with [the shared principles](../principles.md), including §§6.3–6.4 on available context, preservation, and disclosure. Apply section-level completeness checks only when the relevant section is supplied.

**Basis notes:** Original IDs point to the archived guide linked below. They identify practical guidance and provenance, not newly verified empirical findings. “Editorial” identifies a new diagnostic or qualification in this revision. Diagnostic questions are aids to judgment, not a mandatory sequence.

## Purpose

Give a final, proportionate answer to the research question and state the study’s contribution.

## 1. Synthesis

### CO1. Combine findings into an answer

State what the findings establish together rather than summarize each section.

**Synthesis check:** Taking the findings together, what can now be said about the research question? A list of results becomes a synthesis only when their relationship contributes to that answer.

**Basis:** Original section-structure-11; editorial diagnostic drawing on original Discussion section-structure-30.

### CO2. Keep conclusions traceable

Each principal claim must be supported by the body of the manuscript. Synthesis may express a relationship more clearly, but cannot introduce unsupported findings or a broader population claim.

**Traceability check:** Locate the corresponding result or developed interpretation for each concluding claim. If a wider formulation has no corresponding support, narrow it or identify the evidential gap.

When the body is unavailable, assess internal clarity and identify manuscript agreement as unchecked.

**Basis:** Original section-structure-11; editorial diagnostic and context limit.

## 2. Scope and ending

### CO3. Retain consequential qualifications

Include conditions or uncertainty whose omission would materially change the final answer. Avoid repeating every limitation from Discussion.

**Qualification check:** Would the conclusion imply a different level of certainty or applicability without this phrase? Retain it if so.

**Basis:** Editorial extension from shared principles §§1.3 and 6.4 and original Discussion section-structure-3.

### CO4. End with a supported contribution

An implication or research direction belongs when it follows from the findings and gives the reader a specific final message. It is optional.

Keep the form appropriate to a separate Conclusion or the closing part of Discussion. Abstract endings have different space constraints; use [the abstract guide](title-abstract.md) for those.

**Basis:** Original Discussion section-structure-15; editorial placement and optionality. Original Conclusion section-structure-54 is abstract-specific.
