# Introduction and Literature Review

Use with [the shared principles](../principles.md), including §§6.3–6.4 on available context, preservation, and disclosure. Apply section-level completeness checks only when the relevant section is supplied.

**Basis notes:** Original IDs point to the archived guide linked below. They identify practical guidance and provenance, not newly verified empirical findings. “Editorial” identifies a new diagnostic or qualification in this revision. Diagnostic questions are aids to judgment, not a mandatory sequence.

## Purpose

Explain the research problem, establish why it matters, and show how the study addresses it.

## 1. Rationale and contribution

### IN1. Connect context, gap, and need

A useful progression is relevant context → current knowledge → unresolved problem → objective and contribution. A gap can involve an unresolved mechanism, inconsistent evidence, untested conditions, methodological limits, or a meaningful need for replication.

**Gap–consequence check:** What remains unknown, and what explanation or decision is limited by that uncertainty? Absence of a study in a location is not sufficient unless the location matters to the question.

**Basis:** Original section-structure-1, -7, -9; remined-intro-2, -21. Diagnostic: editorial.

### IN2. Align the objective with the study

State the question or objective in terms the design can address. Include hypotheses when they were part of the study; preserve exploratory aims as exploratory.

**Gap–design check:** What feature of the design addresses the stated gap? If the gap concerns a mechanism but the study measures only a pattern, clarify the actual contribution.

Close the section by making the scope and contribution clear. An approach summary or roadmap is useful when it aids navigation or follows venue conventions.

**Basis:** Original section-structure-5, -23, -38, -56; remined-intro-24. Design check and conditional roadmap: editorial.

## 2. Literature use

### IN3. Synthesize by a meaningful comparison

Organize literature around ideas, findings, methods, or disagreements. Select sources for what they support and explain how they establish the problem.

**Synthesis check:** After a group of citations, can the reader state what the studies collectively establish, disagree about, or leave unresolved? If only a list of authors remains, the synthesis is incomplete.

Assess the actual evidence rather than assuming that source type alone establishes consensus.

**Basis:** Original section-structure-18; remined-intro-14, -18, -20. Diagnostic and consensus qualification: editorial.

### IN4. Distinguish embedded and standalone reviews

A literature review within an Introduction supplies the background and rationale needed for this study. A standalone literature-review section may develop a broader conceptual framework or organize a body of evidence.

For a review article, make its scope and relation to existing reviews clear. Search and selection procedures belong with the review’s methods when applicable.

Use relevant project `Knowledge/` material under [the source-use guide](source-use.md).

**Basis:** Original section-structure-32; remined-intro-11, -25. Distinction between review roles: editorial synthesis.

## 3. Paragraph review

### IN5. Judge the paragraph by its role

Identify whether it establishes context, synthesizes literature, develops the gap, gives a rationale, or states an objective.

**Relevance check:** What does this paragraph prepare the reader to understand next? Remove generic background only when its removal leaves the rationale and necessary definitions intact.

Check surrounding text before declaring a section element missing. Match background depth to the intended audience.

**Basis:** Original remined-intro-4, -15, -21, -26; editorial paragraph-level application.
