# Results, Figures, and Tables

Use with [the shared principles](../principles.md), including §§6.3–6.4 on available context, preservation, and disclosure. Apply section-level completeness checks only when the relevant section is supplied.

**Basis notes:** Original IDs point to the archived guide linked below. They identify practical guidance and provenance, not newly verified empirical findings. “Editorial” identifies a new diagnostic or qualification in this revision. Diagnostic questions are aids to judgment, not a mandatory sequence.

## Purpose

Report findings that answer the research questions and provide enough evidence to interpret them accurately.

## 1. Narrative and quantities

### RE1. Establish the comparison

Organize findings by question, analysis, or an explanatory sequence. Orient the reader to the comparison, report the finding, and give the evidence needed to assess it.

**Comparison check:** What is compared with what, over which groups, locations, or times, and in what units? Distinguish changes within groups from differences between groups.

**Basis:** Original section-structure-36, -40; remined-results-6, -8. Diagnostic: editorial.

### RE2. Match wording to data

Report direction, magnitude, variation, and uncertainty as appropriate to the analysis. Identify the sample basis and distinguish dispersion from uncertainty of an estimate.

**Data–statement check:** Do the supplied values support “increased,” “similar,” “higher,” or “no difference”? Distinguish an observed difference, its statistical assessment, and its scientific importance.

Use method-appropriate statistics and justified thresholds. Missing uncertainty cannot be invented during rewriting.

**Basis:** Original section-structure-25; remined-results-1, -7, -8. Semantic distinctions: editorial.

### RE3. Preserve a representative account

Include important null, negative, or unexpected results when they bear on the question.

**Selection check:** Within the supplied material, would omitted findings change the direction, scope, or strength of the conclusion? An excerpt alone cannot establish whether the full study reported selectively.

**Basis:** Original section-structure-36; supporting original Discussion remined-discussion-2 and -6. Diagnostic and excerpt limit: editorial.

## 2. Observation and inference

### RE4. Distinguish a pattern from its explanation

Describing a pattern belongs in clear results reporting. Explaining its cause, relating it to literature, and developing implications usually belongs in Discussion when the sections are separate.

**Illustrative example, not study data:**

- Observation: “Nitrate concentrations were higher at shallow wells.”
- Interpretation: “This pattern may reflect greater exposure to recent agricultural inputs.”

The second sentence requires support for a proposed explanation. In a combined Results and Discussion section, it may follow the observation, provided the distinction and evidential basis remain clear.

Flag inconsistent or unexplained features without assuming their cause or prescribing new sampling automatically.

**Basis:** Original section-structure-28; remined-results-2 and -4, reconciled editorially. Example: editorial.

## 3. Visual evidence

### RE5. Give prose and visuals complementary roles

Use prose to identify the finding and guide attention. Tables support precise comparisons; figures reveal patterns and relationships when suitable.

Repeat selected values when they anchor the argument. Avoid duplicating an entire visual in prose.

**Visual check:** Can the reader locate the cited evidence and identify variables, units, groups, symbols, uncertainty, and sample basis? Check captions and text against supplied visuals.

Follow publication instructions for placement and format.

**Basis:** Original section-structure-8, -37, -41, -46, -62; remined-results-3, -6. Diagnostic and selective-repetition qualification: editorial.

## 4. Paragraph review

### RE6. Distinguish writing repair from additional analysis

Check what the paragraph establishes using the available data. Clarifying a comparison is a writing repair; calculating a new estimate or performing a test is additional analysis and should be identified as such.

**Basis:** Editorial scope boundary.
