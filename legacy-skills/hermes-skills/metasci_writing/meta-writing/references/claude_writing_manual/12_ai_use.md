### `ai-era-1` — convergence 8
Use genAI as an assistant across the whole writing workflow — drafting, language and structure refinement, outlining, summarizing, brainstorming, editing — while the human remains the thinker and author.

> "Artificial intelligence (AI) has expanded the possibilities of improving human-derived text and ideas, serving as a powerful tool to assist in language and readability of text."

### `ai-era-2` — convergence 6
Don't over-rely on AI — keep the deep thinking (judgment, reasoning, argument), oversight, and final accountability yourself; AI complements rather than replaces the author, and over-reliance erodes critical thinking and originality. Handing off the writing means skipping the thinking.

> "So if you hand off writing to LLMs, you’re not just skipping the work.....you’re skipping the thinking"

**Sources (6):** zhu-2026, huang-2023, connellpensky-2025, tweets-01b, tweets-01d, tweets-03
**Scope:** Generative AI Use (thin evidence — read caveat)

### `ai-era-3` — convergence 5
Uphold academic integrity with AI — never fabricate data/citations, generate whole manuscripts without critical review, pass AI output off as your own, or circumvent institutional/journal AI policies; use multiple sources, attribute properly, and rely on a culture of trust and transparency.

> "the use of AI tools does not inherently increase the risk of plagiarism. Rather, the risk of plagiarism depends on how the AI-generated text is used and attributed."

**Sources (5):** huang-2023, tsigaris-2026, tweets-01a, tweets-01b, tweets-01d
**Scope:** Generative AI Use (thin evidence — read caveat)
**[!] Convergence inflated** — 3 of the 5 listed sources are splits of a single Twitter account. Effective independent-source count is 3.

### `ai-era-4` — convergence 4
AI output can look correct but be inaccurate ("hallucination") and stays confident even when wrong — its probabilistic next-token generation makes factual error inherent, so verify every factual claim yourself before use.

> "GenAI can produce output that looks correct but is inaccurate — a phenomenon called \"hallucination.\""

**Sources (4):** connellpensky-2025, huang-2023, tsigaris-2026, zhu-2026
**Scope:** Generative AI Use (thin evidence — read caveat)

### `ai-era-5` — convergence 3
Don't copy-paste or default-accept AI output — critically evaluate, revise, question, select, and integrate it to become a "transformational user"; uncritical use limits benefit and even hinders creativity.

> "students expand and deepen critical thinking and communication competencies through structured conversations with GenAI, becoming \"transformational users\" rather than passive recipients."

**Sources (3):** zhu-2026, connellpensky-2025, tweets-03

### `ai-era-6` — convergence 3
Build genAI literacy and prompting/tool-selection/integration skill — write specific, goal-stated prompts instead of lazy ones, pick the right tool for the task, and train the integration ability itself; lack of literacy is the primary adoption barrier.

> "Stop asking ChatGPT to \"fix my grammar.\" That lazy prompt gets you lazy results."

**Sources (3):** connellpensky-2025, tweets-02, tweets-01d

### `ai-era-8` — convergence 3

> "we found that GPT-4 evaluated its own stories as being significantly more creative than human stories, which is an unexpected finding that merits exploration in future research."

### `ai-era-10` — convergence 2
Never treat AI-detector verdicts as sole or definitive evidence of authorship — detectors produce more false accusations than correct identifications, are biased against non-native English, and cannot catch flawed content; pair them with (also inconsistent) human judgment.

> "the findings of AI detectors are prone to generate more false accusations than correct identifications."

**Sources (2):** tsigaris-2026, tweets-03
**Scope:** Generative AI Use (thin evidence — read caveat)

### `ai-era-11` — convergence 2
Not all AI use is equal — distinguish technical-support, text-development, and transformational tiers; audit yourself by tier (quality) rather than intensity, don't stall at mechanical delegation, and deliberately set the depth of human–machine cognitive distribution per task.

> "If AI is viewed only as a tool (technical support use), it undertakes simple mechanical tasks, making it difficult to trigger a reconfiguration of the cognitive system, so its relationship with research ability is weak."

**Sources (2):** zhu-2026, tweets-03
**Scope:** Generative AI Use (thin evidence — read caveat)

> "\"AI for Research\" (AI4R), as a fifth paradigm of scientific research, emphasizes that AI substantially improves research capabilities through knowledge automation, human–computer intelligence fusion, and solving combinatorial explosion problems."

### `ai-era-15` — convergence 1
With AI the key issue is disclosure, not usage — state which tools were used for which tasks, declare non-use explicitly, and keep AI logs; disclosed use can be permitted and even encouraged.

> "The important issue is disclosure, not usage. If AI use is declared, students and academics should be permitted, and even encouraged, to use it."

**Sources (1):** tsigaris-2026
**Scope:** Generative AI Use (thin evidence — read caveat)

> "GenAI's effectiveness depends on whether a task falls within the tool's capabilities — the \"jagged technological frontier.\""

### `ai-era-20` — convergence 1
Don't assert that deep AI use causes competence — the evidence is correlational and reverse causality is plausible (already-stronger thinkers choose transformational use), so hedge causal claims about AI effects.

> "Cross-sectional data allow identification of associations but make it difficult to establish causality; a reverse-causal possibility exists whereby students with higher critical thinking and research skills are themselves more inclined toward transformative AI use."
