## Choosing and reading the venue

### `remined-submission-1` — convergence 1
Pick three to five target journals **before** starting the research, and design the study to fit their standards and preferred methodologies.

> "Identify 3–5 high-impact journals before starting your research and tailor your study to fit their standards."

**Sources (1):** tweets-04
**Scope:** Journal Submission
**[+] Re-mined 2026-08-10** — from `tweets-04-29`. Goes further than `section-structure-45` (choose the journal before *writing*): this is before the research.

### `remined-submission-2` — convergence 1
Read ten to fifteen papers the target journal published in the last two to five years. Note which topics it prefers, which writing style it favours, and what it implicitly rewards.

> "Read 10-15 papers published in your target journal (last 2-5 years) - Notice what topics they prefer - See what writing style they like - Understand their \"hidden preferences\" This is your roadmap to acceptance."

**Sources (1):** tweets-01d
**Scope:** Journal Submission
**[+] Re-mined 2026-08-10** — from `tweets-01d-25`.

### `remined-submission-3` — convergence 1
A specialist journal and a general one need different amounts and kinds of information — experts in your field versus experts from other disciplines. At minimum, confirm the paper falls within the journal's stated scope.

> "are you targeting a specialist journal, where readers are expected to be experts in your field, or a general medicine journal, where readers may be experts from other disciplines? This will have implications for the amount and type of information that you must include... at the very least, you should check that your paper falls within the scope of the journal you have chosen."

**Sources (1):** ecarnot-2015
**Scope:** Journal Submission
**[+] Re-mined 2026-08-10** — from `ecarnot-2015-7`.

### `remined-submission-4` — convergence 1
Decide which category the paper is submitted as. Most venues accept either original research or survey, or require the author to declare which basis it should be judged on.

> "Papers can be divided roughly into two categories, namely original research papers and survey papers... most publication venues either only accept one or the other type or require the author to identify whether the paper should be evaluated as a research contribution or a survey paper."

**Sources (1):** b5-schulzrinne
**Scope:** Journal Submission
**[+] Re-mined 2026-08-10** — from `b5-schulzrinne-71`.

## Compliance

### `remined-submission-5` — convergence 1
Follow the journal's reference style exactly. Failing to do so is grounds for rejection on its own.

> "You must follow the journal's instructions for authors on this point, at the risk of having your paper rejected if you do not follow the style guidelines."

**Sources (1):** ecarnot-2015
**Scope:** Journal Submission · also relevant to `07_references.md`
**[+] Re-mined 2026-08-10** — from `ecarnot-2015-64`.

### `remined-submission-6` — convergence 1
Do not much exceed the journal's word limit. It is listed among the common reasons manuscripts are returned.

> "The paper much exceeds the maximum number of words allowed"

**Sources (1):** perneger-2004
**Scope:** Journal Submission
**[+] Re-mined 2026-08-10** — from `perneger-2004-27`, recorded in the source's table of common defects.

### `remined-submission-7` — convergence 1
Authorship is reserved for those meeting **all three** conditions: a significant intellectual contribution; drafting or revising the article for intellectual content; and approving the final version, references included.

> "authorship credit must be reserved for individuals who have met each of the following conditions: 1) made a significant intellectual contribution ... 2) contributed to drafting the article or reviewing and/or revising it for intellectual content, and 3) approved the final version of the manuscript, including references."

**Sources (1):** b5-schulzrinne
**Scope:** Journal Submission · also filed under: 08_authorship_acknowledgements.md
**[+] Re-mined 2026-08-10** — from `b5-schulzrinne-21`, quoting IEEE PSPB.

### `remined-submission-8` — convergence 2
Disclose AI use — and disclose non-use. Authors who did not use a language model should say so explicitly. Responsible use means full disclosure plus retained AI logs (prompts and outputs).

> "authors who did not employ LLMs should declare explicitly they did not employ such tools."

## Before you send it

### `remined-submission-9` — convergence 1
Get an internal review — and consider someone *less* familiar with the topic. A reader who knows the area intimately will unconsciously supply the information you left out.

> "Sometimes, a review by someone who is not extremely knowledgeable about the area may be more useful than someone intimately familiar with the topic and who may unknowingly \"read-in\" missing information."

**Sources (1):** b1-iufro
**Scope:** Journal Submission
**[+] Re-mined 2026-08-10** — from `b1-iufro-9`.

### `remined-submission-10` — convergence 1
Run the list of common rejection reasons as a pre-submission self-check: unclear or vague methodology, an unfocused research plan, a mismatch with the sponsor's mission, a budget that does not match the work plan.

> "The methodology or approaches in the experimental section were unclear or vague." / "The research plan was not focused." / "The proposed research did not fit the mission or purpose of the grant program of the funding sponsor." / "The proposed budget does not match the proposal work plan."

**Sources (1):** b1-iufro
**Scope:** Journal Submission
**[+] Re-mined 2026-08-10** — from `b1-iufro-97`.

### `remined-submission-11` — convergence 2
Reread the whole draft once more immediately before submitting. A last pass over typos, leaps, and muddled sentences is what lets reviewers spend their attention on content rather than form. Do not expect a supervisor or committee to copy-edit for you.

> "It is however advisable to reread the draft one more time before submission, as a last-minute correction of typos, leaps, and muddled sentences may enable the reviewers to focus on providing advice on the content rather than the form."

**Sources (2):** pautasso-2013, hon-uf
**Scope:** Journal Submission
**[+] Re-mined 2026-08-10** — from `pautasso-2013-20`, `hon-uf-24`. Second quote: "Do not expect your chair or committee members to copy edit your thesis or dissertation."

### `remined-submission-12` — convergence 1
Build a personal pre-submission checklist that includes the errors *you* habitually make in English, alongside the standard ones (research question, thesis, structure, sources, cohesion, register, spelling, punctuation, formatting).

> "I have double-checked for errors that I tend to make when writing in English."

**Sources (1):** b3-lund
**Scope:** Journal Submission
**[+] Re-mined 2026-08-10** — from `b3-lund-93`.

### `remined-submission-13` — convergence 1
Nothing is ever finished. A deadline is what decides when the paper is done — decide and submit.

> "THE EXTREMELY IMPORTANT LAST STEP: Nothing is perfect, so decide and submit your paper. Usually, a deadline determines when your paper is finished."

**Sources (1):** tweets-02

## After you send it — review, rejection, resubmission

### `remined-submission-14` — convergence 1
The process takes time. Do not read silence as a verdict — but after a reasonable interval (around four weeks), email the editor to ask about status.

> "Unless you are presenting ground-breaking information, the publishing process takes time. Do not become discouraged. If you have not heard from the editor after a reasonable length of time (eg, four weeks), send an e-mail to the editor to ask about your article's status."

**Sources (1):** saver-2007
**Scope:** Journal Submission
**[+] Re-mined 2026-08-10** — from `saver-2007-7`.

### `remined-submission-15` — convergence 1
Know how the decision is actually made. Papers are sorted by average review score: the bottom third is rejected and the top third accepted with little discussion. What gets argued over is the middle band, papers where scores diverge sharply, and papers that drew only two reviews.

> "The technical program chair then collects the reviews and sorts the papers according to their average review scores... Usually, the bottom third and the top third are rejected and accepted, respectively, without (much) further discussion. The papers discussed are those in the middle of the range, or where a TPC member feels strongly that the paper ended up in the wrong bin, or where the review scores differ significantly."

**Sources (1):** b5-schulzrinne
**Scope:** Journal Submission
**[+] Re-mined 2026-08-10** — from `b5-schulzrinne-99`, `b5-schulzrinne-95`, `b5-schulzrinne-98`, `b5-schulzrinne-100`. Describes conference review specifically; journal processes differ.

### `remined-submission-16` — convergence 1
Your reviewer may not be the committee member assigned the paper. It may be a colleague at the same institution, one of their graduate students, or someone listed in your references. Graduate-student reviews often give more detailed criticism than blanket dismissal.

> "They may be colleagues of the reviewer at the same institution, his or her graduate students or somebody listed in the references. The graduate student reviews can be quite helpful, since these reviewers often provide more detailed criticism rather than blanket dismissal."

**Sources (1):** b5-schulzrinne
**Scope:** Journal Submission
**[+] Re-mined 2026-08-10** — from `b5-schulzrinne-96`. Two or three reviews is normal; receiving only two is common (`b5-schulzrinne-97`).

### `remined-submission-17` — convergence 1
**Rejection is a stage, not a verdict.** Revise against the reviews and resubmit — the projects that end up funded are very often resubmissions.

> "Very often the projects that receive funding are those that are re-submissions!"

**Sources (1):** b1-iufro
**Scope:** Journal Submission
**[+] Re-mined 2026-08-10** — from `b1-iufro-11`.

### `remined-submission-18` — convergence 1
Write a resubmission for readers who have not seen the previous round. It will very likely go to different reviewers, so nothing may lean on context established in the earlier submission.

> "Are you aware that your renewal or resubmission will very likely be reviewed by different reviewers?"

**Sources (1):** b1-iufro
**Scope:** Journal Submission
**[+] Re-mined 2026-08-10** — from `b1-iufro-103`.

### `remined-submission-19` — convergence 1
A collaborative proposal must be more than the sum of its parts. Sponsors do not look kindly on a collection of researchers continuing their own work with no real plan for integrating it at every step. Name one Principal Investigator who carries final responsibility, and delineate what each collaborator does and how it contributes to the whole.

> "Research sponsors will not favorably consider a research proposal that is purported to be an integrated group research effort but rather is simply a collection of researchers doing their own work without any real plan or process for integrating the research at every step along the way throughout the life of the research project."

**Sources (1):** b1-iufro
**Scope:** Journal Submission
