### `section-structure-14` — convergence 9
Reference key assertions and the methods used, but do not aim for exhaustiveness, pad the list, or state the obvious — and keep the literature review to the most important studies.

> "References should be used wisely. Key assertions should be referenced, as well as the methods and instruments used. However, unless the paper is a comprehensive review of a topic, there is no need to be exhaustive."

### `section-structure-16` — convergence 8

### `section-structure-63` — convergence 1
Rank sources by quality: peer-reviewed English-language journals first, original research over reviews, the most recent or most reputable when several exist, and avoid websites, personal communications, and unpublished data.

> "you should give precedence to articles published in English-language, peer-reviewed journals... Internet sites should be avoided where possible, as should personal communications and unpublished data. If you have several possible references, you may prefer to choose the most recent one, or the one published in the most reliable and reputed source journal. Try to give priority to original research articles, rather than reviews."

## Citation mechanics

### `remined-references-1` — convergence 1
Do not use "et al." in the bibliography unless the author list is genuinely long — five or more. The name you drop may belong to your supervisor or your reviewer. Note where the full stop goes.

> "Avoid use of et al. in a bibliography unless list is very long (five or more authors)."

**Sources (1):** b5-schulzrinne
**Scope:** References & Citation Ethics
**[+] Re-mined 2026-08-10** — from `b5-schulzrinne-33`, `b5-schulzrinne-85` ("Note punctuation of et al.").

### `remined-references-2` — convergence 1
Put a space between initials and surname — "J. P. Doe", not "J.P.Doe".

> "Leave a space between first names and last name, i.e., 'J. P. Doe', not 'J.P.Doe'."

**Sources (1):** b5-schulzrinne
**Scope:** References & Citation Ethics
**[+] Re-mined 2026-08-10** — from `b5-schulzrinne-37`.

### `remined-references-3` — convergence 1
For a conference paper you **must** give the location, the month, and the conference's full name — not an abbreviation. Page numbers are acceptable but matter less for electronic publication; the bibliographic detail is readily available from the IEEE and ACM digital libraries, so look it up rather than leaving it out.

> "For conference papers, you MUST name the conference location, month and the full conference name, not just some abbreviation."

**Sources (1):** b5-schulzrinne
**Scope:** References & Citation Ethics
**[+] Re-mined 2026-08-10** — from `b5-schulzrinne-39`, `b5-schulzrinne-86`.

### `remined-references-4` — convergence 1
Book citations carry the year of publication and no ISBN.

> "Book citations include publication years, but no ISBN number."

**Sources (1):** b5-schulzrinne
**Scope:** References & Citation Ethics
**[+] Re-mined 2026-08-10** — from `b5-schulzrinne-35`.

### `remined-references-5` — convergence 1
URLs are acceptable, with two cautions. Do not link an author's personal page for a paper published by IEEE or ACM — the copyright position makes it bad form. And avoid long URLs: pointing at the general page and letting the reader find the item survives link rot better.

> "It is now acceptable to include URLs to material, but it is probably bad form to include a URL pointing to the author's web page for papers published in IEEE and ACM publications, given the copyright situation. ... Avoid long URLs; it may be sufficient to point to the general page and let the reader find the material."

**Sources (1):** b5-schulzrinne
**Scope:** References & Citation Ethics
**[+] Re-mined 2026-08-10** — from `b5-schulzrinne-36`.

### `remined-references-6` — convergence 1
A vague citation is a useless one. "Technical report" or "to be published, 2003" identifies nothing — give the issuing source (the university, the body), the date, and whatever else lets a reader locate it.

> "Cite the source (e.g., university), date and other identifying information."

**Sources (1):** b5-schulzrinne
**Scope:** References & Citation Ethics
**[+] Re-mined 2026-08-10** — from `b5-schulzrinne-38`; the source gives two vague-citation examples.

### `remined-references-7` — convergence 1
Mark Internet drafts "work in progress", and check whether a newer version or an RFC has superseded them. Treat any draft reference older than six months as suspect — drafts expire at that point.

> "Internet drafts must be marked 'work in progress'. Make sure that they have been replaced by newer versions or RFCs. Any Internet Draft reference older than six months should automatically be suspicious since Internet Drafts expire after that time period."

**Sources (1):** b5-schulzrinne
**Scope:** References & Citation Ethics
**[+] Re-mined 2026-08-10** — from `b5-schulzrinne-34`. Networking/CS convention; the general principle — check whether a preprint has been superseded — carries across fields.

## What to cite, and what not to

### `remined-references-8` — convergence 1
Know where a citation is *not* wanted: widely known facts and common knowledge, your own original ideas and analysis, and your own empirical findings. Over-citation blurs which contribution is yours.

> "1. When stating widely known facts or common knowledge." / "2. When expressing your own original ideas or analysis." / "3. When presenting your own experimental or empirical findings."

**Sources (1):** tweets-04
**Scope:** References & Citation Ethics
**[+] Re-mined 2026-08-10** — from `tweets-04-30`. The inverse of `section-structure-16`; both are needed, since the failure runs in both directions.

### `remined-references-9` — convergence 1
Cite the sources your hypotheses rest on. Full transparency about where the work came from is an ethical and professional obligation, and citing the basis of a hypothesis is what shows it is sound rather than asserted.

> "It is your ethical and professional responsibility to document your work adequately, and provide full transparency in identifying your sources. It is also imperative to cite the sources on which your hypotheses are based, to prove that they are in fact sound."

**Sources (1):** ecarnot-2015
**Scope:** References & Citation Ethics
**[+] Re-mined 2026-08-10** — from `ecarnot-2015-59`.

### `remined-references-10` — convergence 2
Avoid citing what the reader cannot reach — unpublished work, grey literature such as technical reports, and anything out of date or hard to obtain. Do not pad the list to cite particular authors or journals, and be deliberate about citing your own lab.

> "references to unpublished work, to documents in the grey literature (technical reports), or to any source that the reader will have difficulty finding or understanding should be avoided."

**Sources (2):** perneger-2004, fisher-2013
**Scope:** References & Citation Ethics
**[+] Re-mined 2026-08-10** — from `perneger-2004-24`, `fisher-2013-29`. Second quote: "do not include extraneous references in an effort to simply cite particular authors or journals."

## Handling references while you work

### `remined-references-11` — convergence 2
Record the reference the moment you read it, with the point or phrase you intend to use. You will think you remember where you saw a finding; after dozens of papers you will not, and you will lose real time re-reading everything to find one source.

> "You may think you will remember where you saw such and such a finding, but by the time you have read dozens of papers, your memory may start to flounder, and you may lose significant amounts of time reading through everything again to find one reference."

**Sources (2):** ecarnot-2015, koons-2019
**Scope:** References & Citation Ethics
**[+] Re-mined 2026-08-10** — from `ecarnot-2015-65`, `ecarnot-2015-4`, `koons-2019-21`.

### `remined-references-12` — convergence 1
Use reference-management software. Beyond storage, it renumbers citations automatically when a source is inserted — which is what makes a review article tractable at all.

> "a citation software such as EndNote® or Zotero' is beneficial for writing any manuscript such that reference numbers are automatically reordered upon the addition of a new source, and these tools are especially indispensable for the modern undertaking of writing review articles."

**Sources (1):** koons-2019
**Scope:** References & Citation Ethics
**[+] Re-mined 2026-08-10** — from `koons-2019-21`.

### `remined-references-13` — convergence 2
**Verify every citation a language model gives you against the original.** These systems suggest wrong references and invent ones that do not exist. This is not a hypothetical failure mode — it is the documented one.

> "based on our experience, ChatGPT may suggest wrong references or even cite article references that do not exist; therefore it is extremely important to review and edit the generated citations and references to ensure their accuracy and completeness."

**Sources (2):** huang-2023, connellpensky-2025
**Scope:** References & Citation Ethics · see also 12_ai_use.md
