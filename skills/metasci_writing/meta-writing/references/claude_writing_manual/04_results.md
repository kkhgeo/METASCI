### `section-structure-8` — convergence 10
Present and summarize numeric data in tables and figures, using the text to interpret trends; visuals also carry the reader's interest.

> "Tables and/or figures should be used to illustrate and summarize all numeric information."

### `section-structure-28` — convergence 6
Results presents the data — do not re-explain the methods; discussion, interpretation, and literature comparison belong in the Discussion.

> "The Results section presents the experimental data to the reader, and is not a place for discussion or interpretation of the data."

**Sources (6):** fisher-2013, hon-uf, perneger-2004, b3-lund, hengl-2002, ecarnot-2015

### `section-structure-61` — convergence 1
Methods and Results must map one-to-one — no result for a test not described in Methods, and a result for every method described; previously published procedures need only a brief summary plus a reference.

> "There must be a method described for every result you intend to include in your results section – i.e., you cannot present the results of a test or analysis that was not mentioned in the methods. Conversely, if details of any or all procedures have previously been published elsewhere, then a brief summary will suffice, accompanied by a reference to the relevant publication."

**Sources (1):** ecarnot-2015
**Scope:** Results, Figures & Tables · also filed under: 03_methods.md
**[v] Verified 2026-08-09** — sole source `ecarnot-2015`; quote confirmed verbatim against the source PDF.

### `section-structure-62` — convergence 1
Route each result by form: what fits in one or two lines goes in the text, the same variables across two or more groups go in a table, and data too complex or not easily interpretable go in a figure.

> "results that can easily be described in one or two lines can be written in the text. Tables should be used for data such as baseline characteristics, outcomes, treatments, where the same variables are being described for two or more groups... Figures are useful in cases where the source data is either too complex for presentation or not easily interpretable."

**Sources (1):** ecarnot-2015
**Scope:** Results, Figures & Tables

### `remined-results-1` — convergence 1
Report numerical results and simulations in enough detail that a reader could reproduce them: every parameter used, the number of samples behind each analysis, and any initial conditions.

> "In all but extended abstracts, numerical results and simulations should be reported in enough detail that the reader can duplicate the results. This should include all parameters used, indications of the number of samples that contributed to the analysis and any initial conditions, if relevant."

### `remined-results-2` — convergence 1
Explain anomalies in your own graphs. A dip, a peak, or a change in slope must either be accounted for, or shown with reasons to be statistical aberration — in which case collect more samples.

> "If there's a 'strange' behavior in the graph (e.g., a dip, peak or change in slope), this behavior either needs to be explained or reasons must be given why this is simply due to statistical aberration."

**Sources (1):** b5-schulzrinne
**Scope:** Results, Figures & Tables
**[+] Re-mined 2026-08-09** — from `b5-schulzrinne-44`.

### `remined-results-3` — convergence 1
Choose figures for insight, not coverage. You can never show the whole parameter space, so show which parameters matter over which range — a wall of flat or linear lines tells the reader nothing.

> "Figures should be chosen wisely. You can never lay out the whole parameter space, so provide insight into which parameters are significant over what range and which ones are less important. It's not very entertaining to present lots of flat or linear lines."

**Sources (1):** b5-schulzrinne
**Scope:** Results, Figures & Tables
**[+] Re-mined 2026-08-09** — from `b5-schulzrinne-45`.

### `remined-results-4` — convergence 1
Prose about a figure must interpret it, not restate what is visually obvious. Not "the delay rises with the load" but how that rise relates to the load — linear, threshold, matching a known behaviour.

> "The description of the graph should not just repeat the graphically obvious such as 'the delay rises with the load', but explain, for example, how this increase relates to the load increase."

**Sources (1):** b5-schulzrinne
**Scope:** Results, Figures & Tables
**[+] Re-mined 2026-08-09** — from `b5-schulzrinne-47`. Complements `section-structure-46` (do not repeat table data in the text): that rule says what to cut, this one says what to write instead.
